Weekly, Bi-Weekly or Nightly: Picking a Frequency

Choosing the wrong cleaning frequency is the most common setup mistake in a new recurring contract. Too frequent and you're paying for cleans that aren't needed. Not frequent enough and the facility degrades visibly between visits — which affects both staff morale and client perception. Here's how to think through the decision.

Start with your traffic, not your preference

The right frequency is determined by how many people use the facility, how intensively they use it, and what surface types are involved. A six-person accounting firm and a 40-person insurance company have different needs even if their square footage is similar. Restroom traffic is a reliable proxy — it's the most time-sensitive area and usually the one that shows degradation first.

Weekly cleaning: who it fits

Weekly is appropriate for most commercial offices with 10 or more regular occupants, customer-facing reception areas, and medical or dental facilities with regular patient flow. At weekly frequency, the crew is maintaining a clean facility — the standard from the previous visit is still reasonably intact, and the visit restores it fully.

Weekly is also the right choice if your facility has clients or patients visiting regularly. A waiting area that goes two weeks between cleans will show it. A conference room that hosts client presentations needs to be clean every week, not every fortnight.

Bi-weekly cleaning: who it fits

Bi-weekly works for low-occupancy professional offices — typically under ten regular occupants, few or no clients visiting on site, and limited kitchen or restroom use. Many small professional services firms (one or two principals, an assistant) maintain well on bi-weekly schedules and find weekly visits wasteful.

A bi-weekly schedule can also be appropriate for secondary or back-office spaces that see low traffic — storage areas, filing rooms, seldom-used conference rooms — even if the main office runs weekly.

Nightly cleaning: who it fits

Nightly cleaning is for facilities that generate enough daily use to require a full reset every 24 hours. This includes medical and dental practices with high patient volumes, distribution or fulfillment operations where break rooms and restrooms serve large shift crews, food-adjacent operations, and any facility that simply cannot allow visible degradation to accumulate.

Nightly cleaning is also the answer when your facility operates through the day and has no access window for a standard after-hours weekly crew. If your last employee leaves at 9pm and the first arrives at 6am, nightly is often the only feasible option.

Quarterly deep clean: standalone or layered

A quarterly deep clean doesn't replace a routine schedule — it addresses the buildup that routine maintenance doesn't reach. Most facilities benefit from layering a quarterly deep clean on top of a weekly or nightly contract, timed to lease inspections, seasonal transitions (Mesa's dust season runs through the summer monsoon), or compliance cycles.

What to do if you're not sure

Tell us the square footage, occupancy, and restroom count. Describe what's been happening with your current arrangement — what isn't getting cleaned well, what the interval feels like now. In a single call we can advise on frequency and give you a comparison price for the two or three options that make sense for your facility.
